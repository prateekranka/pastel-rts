## Independently verified source behavior

- `SpriteAtlasCache.loadPack()` increments the generation, calls `disposePendingLoad()`, stages replacement entries, and keeps the active map live while images load (`apps/game-web/src/sandbox/animation/spriteAtlas.ts:70-107`).
- `UnitRenderSystem.hotReload()` clears render batches in the pre-commit callback. The atlas then commits the new entries and disposes old textures (`apps/game-web/src/sandbox/UnitRenderSystem.ts:149-162`, `216-224`). `UnitRenderSystem.dispose()` clears batches before disposing the atlas (`UnitRenderSystem.ts:139-147`).
- A late successful image completion disposes its newly created texture when its generation is stale (`spriteAtlas.ts:182-188`). Cancelled pending entries are disposed by `disposePendingLoad()`.
- The prior re-entrancy concern is not a confirmed public-path leak. A nested public `loadPack()` starts with `disposePendingLoad()`. Public `dispose()` does the same and also disposes active entries. Therefore, when the old load reaches its second generation/identity guard, the prior staged map has already been disposed and cleared. No direct private-field mutation was used as a production trace.
- `replacePack()` delegates to `loadPack()`. The reviewed integrated callers are the constructor load, `hotReload()`, and `dispose()`.

The source under review is exactly `6e4d580` in the review worktree. The review-only commit changes this document only.

## Remaining bounded limitation

`loadPack()` has no cleanup `finally` around `onBeforeCommit?.()`. If an injected callback throws after staging completes, the rejected load can retain its staged map until a later public `loadPack()` or `dispose()` call. The integrated `UnitRenderSystem` callback is the batch-retirement path reviewed here, and no throw was observed. This exceptional callback trace is not a confirmed M1.1 product blocker, but it remains outside the focused test coverage.

This V verdict is scoped to texture ownership and the existing focused test. It does not close the separate Q2, human-design, physical-device, or performance gates.
